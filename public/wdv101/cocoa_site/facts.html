03 / Small details, surprising origins

A remarkable tree behind every bite.

Meet the tropical plant behind cocoa, then follow a few details that make chocolate more interesting.

Meet the cacao tree

Flowers where you might not expect them

Cacao flowers grow directly on its trunk and older branches. This unusual habit is called cauliflory. The fruits develop into the large pods associated with cocoa.

The cacao tree is native to tropical South America. It is now grown in tropical regions around the world. Warmth, moisture, and suitable growing conditions are part of the story behind each harvest.

Inside the fruit are seeds surrounded by pale pulp. The seeds are commonly called cocoa “beans,” even though they come from a tree fruit rather than a bean plant.

From fruit to familiar flavor

Processing changes the ingredient

After harvest, cacao seeds are fermented and dried before later processing. Roasting, removing the shells, and grinding help transform them into ingredients used to make chocolate.

One detail from Harvard’s Nutrition Source: Dutch processing uses alkali to change cocoa’s flavor and appearance, and it can reduce flavanol content.

Why white chocolate looks different

White chocolate contains cocoa butter, sugar, and milk, but no cocoa solids. That missing dark solid portion explains its pale color. Compare it with the cocoa-rich solids discussed on our health page.
