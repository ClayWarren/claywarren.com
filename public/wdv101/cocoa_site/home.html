Before the bar, there was a bean.

Welcome to Sarah’s Cocoa: a small guide to the history, science, and surprising details behind chocolate.

The history of cocoa

Chocolate began as a drink

Chocolate’s story reaches back thousands of years. Archaeologists have identified cacao traces in pottery used by the Mokaya in what is now southern Mexico and Guatemala around 1900 BCE. The vessels point to an early tradition of drinking cacao.

Later Mesoamerican societies, including the Maya and Aztecs, gave cacao important roles in ceremonies, trade, and everyday life. Their chocolate was not the familiar sweet bar: ground cacao could be mixed with water and ingredients such as chilies, vanilla, or corn.

The plant’s scientific name, Theobroma cacao, means “food of the gods.” Learning about those roots adds another layer to a food that is easy to take for granted.

Something more than a sweet

A valuable seed

Cacao beans were valuable enough to serve as currency among the Maya and Aztecs. The Natural History Museum of Utah describes a 16th-century Aztec record listing goods that could be exchanged for cacao beans.

Behind today’s chocolate is a plant, a harvest, and a long cultural history. Continue with cocoa and health or meet the plant in our cocoa facts.
